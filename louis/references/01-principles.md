# Louis principles

Good English UI text helps the reader understand an idea, complete an action, avoid a mistake, or make a decision. If the text does not do one of those, remove it or rewrite it.

## Before you write

Check:

1. Is text needed here at all?
2. Can structure, state, a component, or validation solve the problem instead?
3. What does the user already know?
4. What must they do now?
5. What happens after the action?
6. Do they need to know a deadline, a risk, or a consequence?

## Useful

Write only what is needed at this moment.

Good text:

- gives an action, a result, or a clear state;
- appears at the right time;
- does not repeat obvious component behavior;
- does not explain the product's internal mechanics without benefit to the user;
- does not reveal next steps too early.

Bad: "For your convenience, we've added the ability to…"

Better: "Done", "Request sent", "Couldn't load. Try again".

## Clear

The user must understand what is happening on the screen at a glance, even after losing context.

Test the text against these questions:

- What happened?
- What do I need to do?
- Why does it matter?
- What happens after the action?
- What if it didn't work?

Put the most important thing first: in the heading, the sentence, the paragraph, or the screen.

## Simple

Write short, but never at the cost of meaning. Brevity is good only when the text stays clear.

Simplify:

- long sentences;
- nominalizations ("perform a verification", "make a selection");
- abstract nouns;
- double negatives;
- instructions for obvious interface behavior.

Bad:

- "Submission of the application has been completed"
- "Please enter your phone number"

Better:

- "Application sent"
- "Phone number"

## Tone

Write calmly, neutrally, and to the point. Neutral does not mean cold.

Avoid:

- jokes in transactional flows;
- emotional openings;
- compliments without use;
- pressure;
- excessive positivity;
- apologies instead of a useful explanation.

Bad:

- "Oops! Something went wrong"
- "Unfortunately, we were unable to load your data"

Better:

- "Couldn't load. Try again"
- "We couldn't load your data"

## On the user's level

- Address the user as "you".
- Use contractions: "can't", "we'll", "you're".
- Do not lecture the user.
- Do not promise what the product cannot guarantee.
- Use one term for one thing.
- Do not change meaning for a nicer sound.

## Consistency

One thing — one name. If a flow calls it a "request", do not call it a "submission" later.

Lists, checkboxes, and options must be grammatically and logically parallel.

## No repetition

Do not repeat what the component, heading, or context already shows.

Drop "click", "type", "select", and "enter" when the control makes it obvious.

## Verbs over nouns

Prefer verbs to nominalizations and simple constructions to complex ones.

Bad:

- "Viewing details"
- "Document upload"

Better:

- "View details"
- "Upload documents"

## Modal verbs

Check "can", "may", "must", "should", "need to", "have to", "required". Often a direct action is better.

Bad: "You need to upload your documents."

Better: "Upload your documents."

## Final check

1. Is the text needed right now?
2. Does it help complete the task?
3. Is there a clear action or state?
4. Is there extra emotion, repetition, or jargon?
5. Are the meaning, variables, and placeholders intact?
6. Are numbers, dates, currency, and punctuation correct?
